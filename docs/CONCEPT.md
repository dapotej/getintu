# intu — One-Page Concept

Oct 20, 2026 · @Dapo Tejuoso

## The idea

A paid membership that gives affluent Nigerians and the diaspora curated access to Lagos and Abuja's best clubs, beaches, golf, dining, nightlife and wellness, day and night, with airport perks as a bonus rather than the core. It launches as a **intu December Pass** for diaspora visitors, then becomes an annual membership for residents.

The brand is intu (a play on "get into"; domain getintu.com), and the first product is the intu Lagos December Pass; "Detty December" is used in marketing and hashtags, not as the product name. The model borrows from [DreamFolks Club](https://www.dreamfolks.com/dreamfolksclub.html) in India, but owns its venue relationships rather than routing traffic through other people's inventory.

## Lead product: the intu Lagos December Pass

A 21-day pass, sold in pounds and dollars, that opens the doors diaspora visitors can't open on their own, **day and night**. By day the problem is emptiness: one study of the 2025 season found visitors had [little to do during the day](https://cowrywise.com/detty-december-2025). By night it's access: the best rooms are members-only or guest-list only, like [Lagos Jet Ski Riders Club](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/3228758/lagos-jet-ski-riders-the-club), which needs a member's invitation to get in.

**Who buys it.** Diaspora returnees and their friends, staying two to four weeks, often in groups. The US supplied 27% of international arrivals in 2025, overtaking the UK ([Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/us-top-diaspora-source-lagos-detty-december-2025)). Diaspora visitors drove 55% of the season's ₦396.5bn consumer spend ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/us-uk-top-lagos-detty-december-arrivals-as-diaspora-spending-hits-n396bn/)).

**Why they'd pay.** They have foreign currency but no local connections. Access, not price, is their problem.

## Unit economics and the DreamFolks lesson

A Plus pass at £299 leaves roughly £95 after costs, if members use about 60% of their entitlements. Every cost below is an assumption to test with partners, not a quote.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Meet-and-assist + transfer | £60 | £60 (always used) |
| 4 beach club passes at \~£10 wholesale | £40 | £24 |
| 4 hotel pool/gym passes at \~£15 wholesale | £60 | £36 |
| Golf round | £25 | £15 |
| Spa treatment | £35 | £21 |
| Hosted night at a members-only club | £15 | £9 |
| Departure lounge visit | £20 | £12 |
| Doctor on call (flat fee per pass) | £5 | £5 |
| Concierge, ops, payment fees | £24 | £24 |
| **Total cost** | **£284** | **£206** |
| **Gross margin** | **£15** | **£93** |

Guest-list entry at partner nightclubs is assumed free: clubs earn it back from members' table and bar spend.

Margin depends on unused entitlements, so pricing must hold even at heavy use. Wholesale rates assume partners discount \~40% off retail in return for prepaid volume.

What changed: club visits are fixed allowances (12 Gold, 24 Black); Black spa was halved and meals cut by a third; Gold spa went from 4 to 3; unlimited telemedicine is bought at a flat fee. Gym is now built for people who train several times a week: unlimited gym is bought from partners at a flat monthly corporate rate, so it costs the same whether a member goes twice or twenty times. Prices rise to ₦425,000 (Silver), ₦1.5m (Gold) and ₦3m (Black) to carry it.

Assumptions: per-visit costs are estimates until partners quote; unlimited telemedicine and the visitor doctor-on-call are bought at a flat per-member fee; naira costs convert at roughly ₦1,770/£.

**How we avoid DreamFolks' fate.** DreamFolks routed traffic through lounges it didn't control. Operators then cut it out, and it [shut its domestic lounge business](https://tijorifinance.com/company/dreamfolks-services-ltd/) in September 2025. We avoid this by:

- **Selling direct to members**, so no single bank or card network controls our revenue.
- **Signing exclusive or co-branded deals** with core venues, giving them a reason to stay rather than go direct.
- **Keeping airport lounges a bonus**, bought through an existing network, never the core of the product.
- **Eventually controlling one space**, such as a members' lounge in Lagos, that nobody can take away.

## Risks and the 90-day plan

December 2026 is six weeks away, so treat it as a 100-pass pilot run by hand over WhatsApp, and save the full launch for December 2027.

**Biggest risks**

- **A quieter December 2026.** With general elections two months later, analysts [expect fewer diaspora arrivals](https://www.agusto.com/publications/the-gloss-over-the-cracks-is-nigerias-festive-boom-sustainable/).
- **Competitors.** [Private lifestyle circles](https://theprivatecircles.carrd.co/) already sell curated access in Lagos, and DragonPass's [Basewallet partnership](https://www.dragonpass.com/insights/basewallet-partners-with-dragonpass-to-unlock-affordable-airport-lounge-access-across-africa) targets the same travellers.
- **Venue reliability.** Closures, demolitions and overcrowding in December can break the promise.
- **Members-only clubs saying no.** Night access depends on clubs like Jet Ski Riders agreeing to host non-members; without them, the night offer is just guest lists.

**Plan**

1. **By 31 October:** interview 15 diaspora travellers in the UK and US on price and must-have benefits.
2. **By 15 November:** sign five wave-1 partners on prepaid-voucher terms; launch a landing page taking pounds and dollars; set up the WhatsApp concierge.
3. **15–30 November:** presell through diaspora communities and influencers. Target: 100 passes, about £25,000.
4. **1 December–10 January:** run the pilot; track use per benefit, satisfaction and partner feedback.
5. **By 31 January:** decide on resident tiers. Go if at least 75 passes sold, use lands between 40% and 70%, and four partners agree to renew.

**Open questions:** final name; whether Abuja joins the pilot or waits for 2027; which payment provider settles pounds and dollars to a Nigerian business.
